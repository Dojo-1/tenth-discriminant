# Where an extra prime factor can hide

The original determinant measures a squared volume. Two different losses
can make it divisible by a prime: the rows may fail to fill their lattice,
or the pairing on an already primitive lattice may become singular.
Seeing a singular pairing before removing the lattice defect does not
tell us which loss we are seeing.

This note gives a safe first-layer bound, then identifies what it leaves
open. It is a complete elementary **paper proof**, not a new Lean theorem,
novel valuation principle, production estimate or improved field cutoff.

## The statement and its proof

Let C be an integral n-by-m matrix and B an integral symmetric m-by-m
matrix. Suppose det G is nonzero, where G=C B C^T. For a prime p, write

    r = rank(C mod p),  q = rank(G mod p),  d = n-r,  t = r-q.

Then

    v_p(det G) >= max(2d, n-q) = 2d + max(0, t-d).

No positivity, inertness or invertibility of B modulo p is needed for
this statement. The nonzero determinant assumption is needed to use a
finite valuation.

Work over the local ring Z_(p), whose denominators are prime to p.
Lift a row basis change modulo p to a matrix U with p-unit determinant.
The last d rows of U C are divisible by p. Divide just those rows:

    U C = Delta C1,       Delta = diag(1,...,1,p,...,p).

C1 is integral over Z_(p), but need not yet be primitive. With H=C1 B C1^T,

    U G U^T = Delta H Delta,
    v_p(det G) = 2d + v_p(det H).

In particular the valuation is at least 2d. There is also a useful view
of the remaining rank. Modulo p the last d rows and columns of Delta H
Delta vanish, so the upper r-by-r block of H has rank q. Adjoining d
columns and then d rows increases rank by at most 2d. Thus

    rank(H mod p) <= q+2d,
    nullity(H mod p) >= max(0, n-q-2d) = max(0, t-d).

Local Smith normal form gives v_p(det H) at least that nullity: every
zero Smith entry modulo p represents at least one power of p in the
nonzero determinant. Substitution proves the stated bound. Equivalently,
combine the evaluation-index bound 2d with the usual Gram-nullity bound
n-q; they overlap, so their maximum is justified and their sum is not.

## Two small controls a reader can reconstruct

Take p=3 and B=diag(2,14,2,14), with w=(1,0,1,1), z=(1,0,0,0).
The two rows w,3z have Gram [[18,6],[6,18]] and determinant 288.
Here d=t=1, but v3(288)=2. Adding 2d+t predicts three and is false;
the safe bound gives two. After dividing the second row, the primitive
Gram [[18,2],[2,2]] has determinant 32, a 3-adic unit.

The primitive one-row lattice generated by w has Gram [18]. Its residue
pairing has nullity one but determinant valuation two. Even after true
saturation, radical dimension is a lower bound, not the exact depth.
Replacing the original metric by diag(2,2,2,2) changes 18 to 6 and loses
a valuation layer. Coordinates and their metric must travel together.
