## T0 — naive tropicalization loses determinant cancellations (M; elementary proof)

For p=3 and
    A=[[1,1],[1,4]], B=[[1,1],[1,10]]
all four entry valuations are zero for both positive-definite integral matrices. Thus the tropical determinant formed solely from the entry valuations is 0 for both. But det(A)=3, det(B)=9, so v3(det A)=1, v3(det B)=2 and the coordinate-dependent cancellation defects differ (1 vs 2). Therefore entrywise valuation does not determine the true determinant valuation. This remains true even for real positive-definite matrices.

## T1 — determinant plus mod-p rank does not reconstruct p-primary Smith data (M and P)

Let C=diag(1,3,27) and E=diag(1,9,9). Both have det=81 and rank modulo 3 equal 1. But
    (Delta_1, Delta_2, Delta_3)(C)=(1,3,81)
    (Delta_1, Delta_2, Delta_3)(E)=(1,9,81).
Their 3-adic Smith exponents are (0,1,3) versus (0,2,2).

Independent brute-force enumeration of kernels over (Z/3^m)^3, m=1,2,3, gives
    |ker(C mod 3^m)| = (9,27,81)
    |ker(E mod 3^m)| = (9,81,81).
Thus both are indistinguishable by det and F3 rank, but distinguishable at m=2. This is an exact example of the *fiber of a lossy projection*, not evidence of a new tenth-discriminant exclusion.

## T2 — naive cancellation defect is a coordinate artifact (M)

Take U=[[1,-1],[0,1]] in GL_2(Z), det U=1. Then
    U A U^T = [[3,-3],[-3,4]]
has det 3 and determinantal divisors (1,3), identical to A. The naive cancellation defect changes from 1 to 0, because the tropical determinant of the new presentation is 1. Therefore the defect cannot be treated as intrinsic without retaining a frame or a declared quotient by gauges.

## T4 — valuation extension has a nontrivial fiber (P, hand-checkable, no claim to have constructed a full ordered blueprint)

Work over k=Q with its 3-adic valuation v_3 in additive/min convention. Let

    R = Q[x,y,z,t] / (t - (x*z-y^2)).

For s=4 or s=10, define a k-algebra evaluation ev_s: R -> Q by x=1, y=1, z=s, t=s-1. Composition w_s = v_3 circ ev_s is a valuation on R extending v_3 (with v_3(0)=infinity). It satisfies

    (w_4(x), w_4(y), w_4(z)) = (0,0,0)
    (w_10(x),w_10(y),w_10(z)) = (0,0,0),
    w_4(t)=1,  w_10(t)=2.

Consequently the restriction/coordinate map

    r : Val_{v_3}(R) -> (Z union {infinity})^3,
        w |-> (w(x),w(y),w(z))

is **not injective**: two distinct valuation extensions have identical coarse coordinates but distinct determinant valuations. They also agree on the valuations of every multiplicative monomial in x,y,z. Their distinct values at t arise from additive cancellation.

This is the immediate relevance of Lorscheid's perspective: his scheme-theoretic tropicalization represents extensions of a valuation under the hypotheses of ordered-blueprint theory, rather than decreeing that all extensions with the same coarse coordinates are identical. Recording t as an additional coordinate distinguishes the particular pair, but by itself does not produce a unique inverse map from entry valuations to t. The paper's bend relations and enriched structure should not be oversold as solving this noninjectivity for us. We have **not** built an ordered blueprint for the full integer jet Gram or proved a new scheme-theoretic realization.

## Proven classical filtration identity (P; newly recorded here, not formally compiled)

For G in Mat_n(Z) nonsingular, choose Smith form diag(d_1,...,d_n), d_i|d_{i+1}, and a_i=v_p(d_i). Let h_m=log_p|ker(G mod p^m)|. Then

    v_p(Delta_k(G))=sum_{i<=k} a_i,
    h_m=sum_i min(a_i,m),
    h_m-h_(m-1)=#{i:a_i>=m}.

Proof: Smith transformations are integral unimodular, hence bijections modulo p^m. The equation d_i z=0 mod p^m has exactly p^{min(a_i,m)} solutions; take products. The determinantal divisors are products of the first k Smith entries. Therefore both full profiles recover the multiset of p-adic depths; rank mod p and total determinant generally do not.
