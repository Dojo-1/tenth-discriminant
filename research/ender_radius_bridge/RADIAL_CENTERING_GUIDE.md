# Change the ruler; keep the arithmetic object

**A candidate argument, not a new checked cutoff.** The published bound
remains50 billion. The fixed paper target is48 billion.

Our determinant measures the collective independence of polynomial
observations. A diagonal upper bound measures each observation separately
and can waste information about how the observations fit together.

We can change those observations without changing the determinant.
For example, replace Z^2 W by Z^2 W-aZ. More generally, replace

$$Z^rW^s\quad\text{by}\quad
Z^{r-m}W^{s-m}(ZW-a)^m,\qquad m=\min(r,s).$$

The new row is its old row plus integer multiples of earlier rows.
The resulting matrix has diagonal 1 and an integer inverse. This preserves
the determinant, integral lattice, determinant divisibility and ranks
modulo every prime. We keep the same points and all ordinary derivatives. This preserves a
linear space of observations; it does not preserve polynomial multiplication.

The geometric choice is a=floor(rho^2/2). On the whole sampled disc,
ZW becomes the real radius squared. Centering that quantity reduces its
largest distance from the center to approximately half the old range.

Differentiation could destroy this benefit. We therefore keep EVERY
Leibniz term. Counting which of r factors get differentiated gives a
precise bound: in rows r>=5S, all sampled derivatives retain a factor
at most(5/8)^s relative to the old radial envelope. Other rows retain
the old envelope. An individual actual row norm can still increase;
the claim is about the uniform geometric bound.

Summing the resulting logarithmic savings gives at least 0.013 after
normalization by R^2 S, under the production dimension guards. The old
arithmetic lower bound stays unchanged. At the fixed candidate endpoint
48 billion, the paper comparison has exact rational margin 0.0031862561.
