## The exact mathematical object

Let \`p\` be an odd witness prime, inert under the original class-number-one reductio, and \`k=F_(p²)=F_p[ω]\` with \`ω²=−D\`. A lattice observation \`q=(x,y)\` becomes \`a=x+yω\`, whose Frobenius satisfies \`a^p=x−yω\`. The original **ordinary** partial derivative is
\[
\partial_Z^j(Z^rW^s)=(r)_{\underline j}Z^{r-j}W^s
=j!\binom rj Z^{r-j}W^s,
\]
with the \(j>r\) case defined to be zero. The integer-pair source [\`integerDerivativeRow\`](../../lean/TenthDiscriminant/IntegerTraceGram.lean) and unchanged \`2(u_\mathrm{re}v_\mathrm{re}+D\,u_\mathrm{im}v_\mathrm{im})\` trace pairing define **one** production Gram. ## The precise Frobenius jet-square

On the affine surface \(X=\operatorname{Spec}k[Z,W]\), let \(\Gamma_p\) be the Frobenius graph \(W=Z^p\), and let \(a\in k\). For \(1\le J\le p\), there is an exact isomorphism of length-\(J\) local algebras
\[
\boxed{\quad
k[Z,W]/(W-a^p,(Z-a)^J)
\;\overset{\Gamma_p^*}{\longrightarrow}\;
k[T]/(T-a)^J,\qquad Z\mapsto T,\ W\mapsto T^p.
\quad}
\]
The pullback is well-defined because \((T^p-a^p)=(T-a)^p\) vanishes modulo \((T-a)^J\). It is an isomorphism: both quotients are generated by the class of the same nilpotent coordinate \(Z-a\), and have basis \(1,h,\dots,h^{J-1}\). This is a **directional** fat point, not the two-variable maximal-ideal power \(\mathfrak m^J\).

Equivalently, for \(F\in k[Z,W]\) and \(j<J\le p\),
\[
[h^j]F(a+h,a^p)
=
[h^j]F(a+h,(a+h)^p).
\]
These are Hasse/Taylor **coefficients**. The original ordinary derivatives satisfy \(\partial_Z^jF(a,a^p)=j!\,[h^j]F(a+h,a^p)\). Because \(j<p\), \(j!\in k^\times\). This is a one-prime rank equivalence, NOT an integral isometry and NOT permission to change the Gram.

**Precision boundary [explicit counterworld]:** at \(J=p+1\), the map is not well-defined: \(W-a^p\) is zero in the left directional quotient but maps to \(h^p\ne0\) on the graph. For \(F=W\), its \(\partial_Z^p\) is zero while its *graph Taylor coefficient* at \(h^p\) is 1. Ordinary derivatives of order \(\ge p\) vanish on \(\mathbf F_p[T]\), so they do not test length-\(J>p\) multiplicity. Split/ramified primes and characteristic 2 also lack the invertible inert conjugation-channel map.

For a conjugate-stable set of residues \(\Xi\), define its monic annihilator
\[
g_Q(T)=\prod_{(x,0)\in Q}(T-x)
\prod_{\substack{(x,y)\in Q\\y>0}}\big((T-x)^2+Dy^2\big)
\quad\in\mathbf F_p[T].
\]
If all residues are distinct, the CRT identifies the sum of length-\(S\) local jets with \(k[T]/g_Q(T)^S\) (after extension of scalars from \(\mathbf F_p\)). The original *rectangular* \(\{Z^rW^s\}\) maps to the classes of \(\{T^{r+ps}\}\). Thus its full-rank test can be performed **independently** in the polynomial quotient, not just by eliminating the same integer matrix twice.

## A sharp jet theorem that explains the role of precision [P-paper, not production radius]

For *any odd prime p*, \(R=p-1\), and \(1\le S=J\le R\), sample **all nonzero real residues** \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\). The \(RS\times RS\) ordinary jet matrix has entries
\[
E_{(r,s),(a,j)}=(r)_{\underline j}a^{r+s-j}.
\]
Multiplying column (a,j) by \(a^j\in\mathbf F_p^\times\) and Fourier-transforming the \(p-1\) points decomposes it by \(t=r+s\bmod(p-1)\). Each block has rows \(r_s=(t-s)\bmod(p-1)\) and columns \(j=0,\dots,S-1\), with entries \((r_s)_{\underline j}\). That falling-factorial Vandermonde determinant \(\prod_{u<v}(r_v-r_u)\) is nonzero mod p, since the \(r_s\) are distinct in \([0,p-2]\). **Thus full rank is attained using exactly p−1 real roots and J=S ordinary jets.** Value-only observations cannot do this for S>1. Finite tests run p=3,5,7,11,31 and the stated S cases, including 150×150.

This algebraic jet theorem does **not** meet the competitive ellipse radius: p−1 distinct real residues require representatives with \(\rho\gtrsim p/2\), whereas our production aim has \(\rho=O(D^{3/4})\ll p\asymp D\).

## The cross-prime integer remainder is substantial

The source matrix of **ordinary** real/imaginary channels factors exactly as \(E_{\rm ord}=E_{\rm Hasse}F\), with \(F\) diagonal containing \(j!\) once for each effective integer channel. At the witness p>J it is invertible mod p. At smaller valuation primes q≤J−1 it is not. For all three D163 *square effective-channel* supports above, each derivative order j=0..4 occurs exactly 30 times. Therefore, over the integers,
\[
\det E_{\rm ord}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^4j!\right)^{30}\det E_{\rm Hasse}=288^{30}\det E_{\rm Hasse}.
\]
For the corresponding positive trace Grams with **the same effective diagonal metric**, 
\[
\boxed{\det G_{\rm ord}=288^{60}\det G_{\rm Hasse}
=2^{300}3^{120}\det G_{\rm Hasse}.}
\]
This formula relies on an **exactly square effective-channel matrix**; for a rectangular oversampled matrix, Cauchy–Binet reweights individual minors and a single global factorial ratio does not follow. Mod31, the factor happens to equal 1 by Fermat's theorem, so both square determinant residues agree, despite the massive integer discrepancy.

Direct independent finite-rank tests for the same 15-node D163 support: modulo 2, original/Hasse ranks 8/20; modulo 3, 18/30; modulo 5, 50/50; modulo 31, 150/150. The p=2 claim here concerns the *integer observation matrices*, not a nondegenerate Fp² inert field. A rank witness at 31 therefore cannot authorize an integral substitution at the primes supplying the determinant mass.

The moving integral radial frame \(\mathrm{RadialOperator.polynomial}(a,W,r,s)=Z^{r-\min(r,s)}W^{s-\min(r,s)}(ZW-a)^{\min(r,s)}\) was checked in **5,040** source-parameterized derivative cases by direct univariate differentiation with W held constant versus the integral monomial-frame expansion. Both agree; the radial basis remains unitriangular and does not rescale away \(j!\).
