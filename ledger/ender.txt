1. Scope and inherited mathematics

D means the absolute FUNDAMENTAL discriminant of an imaginary quadratic
field, not an arbitrary radicand. For D>163, h(-D)=1 implies D is prime,
D=3 mod 8, and every prime p<(D+1)/4 is inert. One elementary proof:
a nonrational element (a+b*sqrt(-D))/2 has norm >=(D+1)/4. A split or
ramified prime ideal of smaller norm would be principal and contradict
this bound. Even fundamental discriminants >8 are excluded by the
ramified prime 2 and x^2+m*y^2=2. A composite odd fundamental D>163
has a prime divisor <=sqrt(D)<(D+1)/4, excluding ramification; inertness
of 2 then gives D=3 mod 8. No classification table is used.

Keep Green's derivative Gram determinant and the ellipse
{x+y*sqrt(-D): x,y integers, x^2+D*y^2<=rho^2}. The inherited lemmas are:
 n >= pi*rho^2/sqrt(D)-2*rho/sqrt(D)-6*rho,
 n <= pi*rho^2/sqrt(D)+2*rho+M,
 L=2*floor(rho)+1, M=2*floor(rho/sqrt(D))+1.
Rows are independent if J>=L+M and nJ>2RS+J(R+S).
For odd inert p, v_p(det G)>=2F(p),
 F(p)=sum_{r=0}^{R-1} sum_{t=0}^{S-1} min(floor(r/p),t).
Hadamard's logarithmic upper bound U is implemented by upper_B.
These non-elementary-in-size determinant arguments still need eventual
formalization. Ender replaces only the prime-distribution input.

2. Factorials replace Dusart

Set A(q)=sum_{r<R} floor(r/q), V=R(R-1)/2, and
W=R(R-1)(2R-1)/6. If R=kq+m, 0<=m<q, then
A(q)=q*k*(k-1)/2+k*m.

For H=log(prod_{r=1}^{R-1} r!), Legendre's identity gives
 H=sum_p log(p)*sum_{j>=1} A(p^j).
This is a finite exact identity, not an asymptotic theorem about primes.
Let N=R-1. Integrating log(r!)>=r*log(r)-r+1 and the increasing
function t*log(t) on [1,N] gives
 H >= Hlow = (N^2/2)*log(N)-3*N^2/4+N/2+1/4.

Fix P=10000 and enumerate only primes <=P. Define
 Bsmall=sum_{p<=P} log(p)*sum_{j>=1} A(p^j).
Every summand is computed with exact integer coefficients and Arb logs.
For integer m>P,
 log(m)/(m*(m-1)) <= integral_{m-1}^m log(t+1)/t^2 dt.
Indeed log(t+1)>=log(m) throughout that interval.
Consequently both
 sum_{p>P} log(p)/(p*(p-1)) and sum_{p>P} log(p)/p^2
are at most
 I(P)=integral_P^infinity log(t+1)/t^2 dt
     =log(P+1)/P+log(1+1/P).
Since A(q)<=V/q, the higher prime powers for p>P cost at most V*I(P).
Thus T=sum_{p>P} log(p)*A(p) >= Hlow-Bsmall-V*I(P).

For P>=R/S and p>P, k=floor(r/p)<S, so
 sum_{t<S} min(k,t)=(S-1/2)*k-k^2/2.
Using sum_r floor(r/p)^2 <= W/p^2 yields
 sum_{p>P} log(p)*F(p)
 >= (S-1/2)*(Hlow-Bsmall-V*I(P))-W*I(P)/2.
Take the maximum of this bound and zero, add the EXACT contributions
log(p)*F(p) for 2<p<=P, and multiply by two. This is Ender's lower
bound. It omits p=2 throughout the determinant lower bound, while
correctly subtracting its powers from Legendre's identity.

4. Universal closure, not a point probe

For ALL real D>=D0=10^36 choose R=floor(D/4), C=80, S=J=floor(R/C),
rho=ceil(1.001*sqrt((3R+S)*sqrt(D)/pi)+10*sqrt(D)).
Only integer D are relevant. Write u=log(D), I=I(P), and
 B=sum_{p<=P} log(p)/(p-1)+I.
We have R>=D/5, R<=D/4, S>=R/(2C), R/S<=2C<=P, and
 u-log(4)-5/D <= log(R) <= u.
All these hold already for D>=D0. The log bound follows from
log(1-4/D)>=-5/D for D>=20.

Using Bsmall<=V*sum_{p<=P} log(p)/(p-1) and dropping positive exact
small-prime contributions,
 L/(R^2*S) >= u-log(4)-3/2-B-(2C/3)*I-E_L(u),
 E_L(u)=[5+5*(2u+2)+5C*u]/exp(u).
Details: 2Hlow/R^2>=log(R)-3/2-(2log(R)+2)/R, obtained by
log(R-1)>=log(R)-2/R and (R-1)^2/R^2>=1-2/R.
The factorial-tail bracket is positive at u0 and increases thereafter.
Its value is at most u; multiplying by (1-1/(2S)) loses at most
(C/R)*u<=5C*u/D. Also W/(R^2*S)<=R/(3S)<=2C/3.
These inequalities apply to the unclipped tail; clipping it at zero
can only improve the lower bound.

The radius satisfies rho<=D^(3/4), because
 1.001*sqrt((3+1/C)/(4*pi))+10*D^(-1/4)+2*D^(-3/4)<1.
The extra 2 also accommodates the finite-precision integer selection
in the interval code, which checks that its chosen radius is less than
the unrounded target plus 2. For the universal proof, the exact ceiling
itself costs at most 1.
Also R>rho, n_up<4D, and
 U/(R^2*S) <= (3/4+5/(4C))*u+E_U(u),
 E_U(u)=5*(log(8)+2u)/exp(u)
        +(2/C)*(5*exp(-u/4)+5u/exp(u)).
To see this, normalize upper_B, bound (S-1)/R<=1/C, and use
 (1+1/C)log(rho)+(2/C)log(R/rho)
 =(1-1/C)log(rho)+(2/C)log(R).
Drop the helpful negative derivative term. Bound the remaining
rho/R and log(R/rho)/R terms by 5*D^(-1/4) and 5u/D.
For n_up<4D use pi*D+2D^(3/4)+2D^(1/4)+1<4D at D0;
after division by D all error terms decrease.

Geometry holds throughout: need=3R+S>=3D/5.
The 1.001^2 factor gives slack .002001*need; negative point-count
errors are at most 8D^(3/4). Hence it suffices that
 .002001*(3/5)*D^(1/4)>8.
Likewise J>=D/(10C) and L+M<=6D^(3/4), so D^(1/4)>60C suffices.
The script checks these stronger sufficient conditions.

Therefore
 (L-U)/(R^2*S) >= (15/64)*u
   -[log(4)+3/2+B+(2C/3)*I]-E_L(u)-E_U(u).
Every error is a positive combination of exp(-u/4), exp(-u), and
u*exp(-u), decreasing for u>=u0>1. The radius, line and count
conditions improve with D. Thus positivity at u0 proves positivity
for ALL u>=u0, despite the floors in R and S.
The certified lower margin at D0 is about 7.84016012450.
